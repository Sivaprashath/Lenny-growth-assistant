# Episode 6: Gustaf Alstromer — Why Retention is the Only Metric That Matters and YC Growth Secrets
Guest: Gustaf Alstromer (Group Partner at Y Combinator, Former Head of Growth at Airbnb)
Host: Lenny Rachitsky

## Transcript Excerpts

Lenny Rachitsky: Gustaf, you have evaluated thousands of startups at Y Combinator and helped Airbnb grow from a small startup to an international powerhouse. When you look at an early stage company, what is the single most diagnostic metric you look at?

Gustaf Alstromer: It is always retention. Nothing else comes close. If a founder comes to me and says: "We have 100,000 signups, we grew 50% month-over-month, and our CAC on Meta ads is $12", the very first thing I ask is: "Show me your cohort retention curve."

If the retention curve slopes straight down to zero at Month 3, the company is dying. They just don't know it yet because top-of-funnel acquisition is masking the rot. A leaky bucket can look like it's full as long as the water faucet is turned on full blast, but the moment you turn down marketing spend, the bucket empties.

Lenny Rachitsky: What does a healthy retention curve actually look like?

Gustaf Alstromer: A healthy retention curve always has a "flat tail".
It might drop sharply in the first few days or weeks—say from 100% to 40%—which is normal as non-target users bounce. But between Month 2 and Month 12, it must flatten out into a horizontal asymptote.

For consumer social (like Instagram or TikTok), good D30 retention is 30% - 40%.
For B2B SaaS (like Slack or Notion), annual net revenue retention (NRR) should be 110% to 130%, meaning expansion from surviving cohorts exceeds lost revenue from churned customers.
For marketplaces (like Airbnb or Uber), annual user retention of 25% - 35% with high repeat transaction value is world-class.

Lenny Rachitsky: How should founders determine their North Star Metric?

Gustaf Alstromer: Your North Star Metric (NSM) must capture the moment of core value delivery, not revenue directly. Revenue is a lagging indicator.
At Airbnb, the North Star Metric was "Nights Booked."
At Facebook early on, it was "Monthly Active Users" who connected with at least 10 friends in 14 days.
At Spotify, it is "Time Spent Listening."
At Slack, it is "Messages Sent within Teams."

If your North Star Metric goes up, customer value went up, which inevitably drives retention and revenue. If you pick a superficial metric like page views or app downloads, your team will game the metric with gimmicky push notifications that destroy long-term trust.
