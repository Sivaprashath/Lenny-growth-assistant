# Episode 3: Shreyas Doshi — High Agency, The LNO Framework, and How Great PMs Think
Guest: Shreyas Doshi (Former VP of Product at Stripe, Twitter, Google, Yahoo)
Host: Lenny Rachitsky

## Transcript Excerpts

Lenny Rachitsky: Shreyas, your frameworks have changed the way thousands of product managers think. One of the concepts you talk about that resonates most deeply is "High Agency." How do you define High Agency, and why is it the defining trait of top 1% PMs?

Shreyas Doshi: High agency is the ability to bend reality to your will. When a low agency person faces an obstacle—such as "Legal said no", "The API doesn't support that", or "Engineering doesn't have bandwidth"—they treat the obstacle as a final immutable law of nature. They report the blocker and wait.

A high agency person looks at an obstacle and asks: "What must be true for this to happen anyway?" They understand that almost all corporate constraints are manufactured by humans and can therefore be renegotiated by humans. They find alternative paths, they build low-fidelity prototypes over the weekend, they schedule 15 minutes with the General Counsel to re-frame the risk, and they solve the problem. In product management, you have zero formal authority over engineering, design, or sales. High agency is your only genuine superpower.

Lenny Rachitsky: Let's talk about the LNO Framework for time management and prioritization. How should product leaders apply it?

Shreyas Doshi: Most PMs are drowning in exhaustion because they give equal effort to every task on their calendar. That is a recipe for burnout and mediocrity. The LNO framework divides your tasks into three categories:

1. **L: Leverage Tasks (10x Impact)**
These are decisions and artifacts where the difference between great and good has massive, enduring consequences. Examples: defining your product strategy, designing your core pricing model, hiring senior engineering leaders, writing a crisp PRD for your next major initiative. On Leverage tasks, you must invest perfectionism and deep focus. You spend hours refining the details.

2. **N: Neutral Tasks (1x Impact)**
These are standard operational duties. If you do them brilliantly, nobody notices; if you do them poorly, things break. Examples: weekly status updates, sprint planning hygiene, standard customer interview synthesis, routine cross-functional alignment. Do them competently, efficiently, and quickly. Never over-engineer an N task.

3. **O: Overhead Tasks (<0.1x Impact)**
These are administrative chores that must get done to keep the organization running: expense reports, mandatory compliance training, filling out bureaucratic feedback forms. Spend the absolute minimum time on O tasks. Delegate them, batch them into a 30-minute block on Friday afternoon, or automate them.

If you spend 3 hours polishing a routine status report, you are stealing time from your high-leverage product strategy. Top PMs consciously calibrate their effort to task leverage.

Lenny Rachitsky: What is the most common failure mode in Product Requirements Documents (PRDs)?

Shreyas Doshi: Writing a PRD as a feature catalog rather than a problem diagnosis. A bad PRD lists 40 bullet points of buttons, drop-downs, and edge cases. A great PRD explains:
1. What is the customer problem, and how do we know it hurts?
2. What are the non-goals? (Knowing what NOT to build is harder than knowing what to build).
3. What is the hypothesis, and how will we know if we succeeded?
4. What are the key architectural and user journey trade-offs?

If your team doesn't understand *why* they are building something, giving them detailed specifications will only help them build the wrong thing faster.
